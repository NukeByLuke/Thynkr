Risk Management and the Insurance Safety Net

Wealth building is only effective if it is protected against unforeseen catastrophes and liability. This file examines the various layers of the insurance safety net, including life, health, and disability insurance, which is often overlooked by younger workers but is vital for protecting one's future earning potential. It discusses the nuances of Auto Insurance and Tenant's Insurance, explaining how deductibles and coverage limits affect monthly premiums and total protection. By treating insurance as a tool for risk transfer, learners can ensure that a single accident or illness does not derail years of disciplined saving and investing.

Insurance Philosophy:

Core Principle: Transfer Risk You Can't Afford
- Insurance is not an investment
- It's catastrophe protection
- Pay small premium to avoid massive loss
- Only insure what would devastate you financially

Self-Insure Small Risks:
- High deductibles lower premiums
- Emergency fund covers small claims
- Save on premiums, self-insure minor losses

Types of Insurance:

1. Health Insurance:

Provincial Coverage (Canada):
- Doctor visits, hospital stays
- Emergency services
- Diagnostic tests
- Free at point of service

What's NOT Covered:
- Prescription drugs
- Dental care
- Vision care
- Paramedical services (physio, massage, etc.)
- Ambulance (partially covered)

Extended Health Insurance:
- Often through employer (80-100% coverage)
- Covers prescriptions, dental, vision
- Paramedical services (limits apply)
- Out-of-country emergency coverage
- Students: check if university offers plan

2. Disability Insurance:

Purpose: Replace Income if Unable to Work
- Most valuable insurance for young people
- Your ability to earn is your greatest asset
- 1 in 4 workers will be disabled before retirement

Types:
- Short-term: 3-6 months coverage
- Long-term: to age 65
- Own-occupation vs any-occupation
- Group (employer) vs individual policy

Coverage Amounts:
- Typically 60-70% of gross income
- Benefits are tax-free if you pay premiums
- Taxable if employer pays premiums
- Waiting period: 90-180 days typical

Cost:
- 1-3% of annual income
- Cheaper when young and healthy
- Women pay more (higher claim rates)
- Group plans cheaper than individual

Who Needs It:
- Anyone relying on income to live
- Self-employed (no employer coverage)
- Single income households
- Those without significant savings

3. Life Insurance:

Purpose: Replace Income for Dependents
- Only needed if someone relies on your income
- Not needed if single with no dependents
- Not needed if financially independent

Types:
- Term Life: Coverage for set period (10, 20, 30 years)
  - Cheapest option
  - No cash value
  - Sufficient for most people
  
- Whole Life: Permanent coverage with cash value
  - Much more expensive
  - Investment component (often poor returns)
  - Complex and usually unnecessary
  
- Universal Life: Flexible permanent insurance
  - Combines insurance and investing
  - Even more complex
  - Better to separate insurance and investing

Coverage Amount:
- 10-15x annual income rule of thumb
- Cover mortgage, debts, education costs
- Enough for spouse to replace your income
- Less needed as wealth accumulates

Cost (Term Life Example):
- $500,000, 20-year term
- Healthy 25-year-old: $20-30/month
- Healthy 35-year-old: $30-50/month
- Premiums increase with age and health risks

When to Get It:
- Marriage
- Buying a home
- Having children
- Starting a business

4. Auto Insurance (Ontario Context):

Mandatory Coverage:
- Third-Party Liability: $200,000 minimum (recommend $1-2M)
- Accident Benefits: Medical and rehab for you and passengers
- Direct Compensation Property Damage: Repairs if not-at-fault
- Uninsured Automobile: Protection from uninsured drivers

Optional Coverage:
- Collision: Repairs to your car regardless of fault
- Comprehensive: Non-collision damage (theft, vandalism, weather)
- Loss of Use: Rental car while yours is repaired
- Depreciation Waiver: New car replacement within 2 years

Cost Factors:
- Age and driving experience
- Vehicle type and value
- Location (postal code)
- Driving record (tickets, accidents)
- Annual mileage
- Deductible amount

Cost Reduction Strategies:
- Increase deductible ($500 → $1,000)
- Bundle with home/tenant insurance
- Group rates (alumni, professional associations)
- Winter tire discount
- Pay annually vs monthly
- Good student discount
- Telematics programs (track driving)

5. Tenant's Insurance (Renter's Insurance):

What It Covers:
- Personal property (furniture, electronics, clothing)
- Liability (someone injured in your rental)
- Additional living expenses (hotel if unit uninhabitable)
- May cover bike theft, even off premises

What It Doesn't Cover:
- Building structure (landlord's responsibility)
- Roommate's belongings (they need own policy)
- Floods (separate coverage needed)
- Normal wear and tear

Cost: $15-30/month typical
Coverage: $20,000-50,000 personal property

Why You Need It:
- Landlord's insurance doesn't cover your stuff
- Liability protection is critical
- Very affordable peace of mind
- May be required by lease

6. Critical Illness Insurance:

What It Covers:
- Lump sum payment upon diagnosis
- Cancer, heart attack, stroke typically
- Use for anything (not just medical)

Who Needs It:
- Self-employed with no paid sick leave
- Those with family history of illness
- Income protection beyond disability insurance

Cost:
- More expensive than term life
- Increases significantly with age
- Consider if high-risk factors

Insurance Mistakes to Avoid:

1. Being Underinsured:
   - Cheap premiums, inadequate coverage
   - Saving $10/month, risking $100,000 loss
   - Review coverage annually

2. Being Over-Insured:
   - Whole life insurance when term sufficient
   - Low deductibles (expensive premiums)
   - Coverage on things you can afford to replace

3. Mixing Insurance and Investing:
   - Whole/Universal life policies
   - High fees eat returns
   - Better: Buy term, invest difference

4. Not Shopping Around:
   - Premiums vary widely between companies
   - Get 3-5 quotes every 2-3 years
   - Use broker for multiple quotes

5. Letting Coverage Lapse:
   - Missing payments voids coverage
   - Much harder to get new policy
   - Set up auto-pay

Insurance Checklist for Students/Young Professionals:

Age 18-25, Single:
- ✓ Health insurance (employer/school/provincial)
- ✓ Auto insurance (if you drive)
- ✓ Tenant's insurance
- ✗ Life insurance (no dependents)
- ✗ Disability insurance (unless high income)

Age 25-35, Married/Common-Law, No Kids:
- ✓ Health insurance (extended)
- ✓ Auto insurance
- ✓ Home/Tenant insurance
- ✓ Term life insurance ($500K-$1M)
- ✓ Disability insurance (especially if one income)

Age 25-35, With Children:
- ✓ All of the above
- ✓ Increase life insurance ($1M+)
- ✓ Both spouses have life insurance
- ✓ Disability insurance critical

Self-Employed:
- ✓ Private health insurance
- ✓ Disability insurance (crucial)
- ✓ Term life insurance
- ✓ Liability insurance (if applicable)
- ✓ Emergency fund (minimum 6 months)

Emergency Fund vs Insurance:

Emergency Fund Covers:
- Job loss
- Small to medium unexpected expenses
- Deductibles and co-pays
- Income gaps

Insurance Covers:
- Catastrophic health issues
- Total disability
- Death (dependents' income replacement)
- Major liability claims
- Major property damage

You Need Both:
- Insurance for catastrophes
- Emergency fund for everything else
- Together they create financial safety net
