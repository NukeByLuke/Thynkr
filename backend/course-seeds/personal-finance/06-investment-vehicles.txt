Investment Vehicles: TFSAs, RRSPs, and Beyond

Building long-term wealth in Canada necessitates the use of tax-advantaged accounts to protect growth from the CRA. This file provides a deep dive into the Tax-Free Savings Account (TFSA), emphasizing its flexibility and tax-free withdrawal status, and the Registered Retirement Savings Plan (RRSP), which offers immediate tax deductions but deferred taxation on withdrawal. It introduces the concept of Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) and Index Funds as low-cost, diversified ways to capture broad market growth. The module stresses Asset Allocation, explaining how to balance risk and reward based on an individual's time horizon and personal risk tolerance.

Tax-Advantaged Accounts:

TFSA (Tax-Free Savings Account):

Contribution Rules:
- Annual limit: $7,000 (2026)
- Cumulative room since 2009 (when you turned 18)
- Unused room carries forward forever
- Withdrawal room returns next calendar year

Tax Benefits:
- Contributions not tax-deductible
- Growth is tax-free
- Withdrawals are tax-free
- No income reporting required

Best Uses:
- Emergency fund (once built)
- Short to medium-term goals (house, car, wedding)
- Long-term investing (especially if low income)
- Already maxing RRSP

What You Can Hold:
- Cash, GICs, bonds
- Stocks, ETFs, mutual funds
- Options (if broker allows)
- Some restrictions on foreign stocks

RRSP (Registered Retirement Savings Plan):

Contribution Rules:
- 18% of previous year's earned income
- Maximum: $31,560 (2026)
- Unused room carries forward indefinitely
- Reduces current year's taxable income

Tax Benefits:
- Contributions are tax-deductible
- Growth is tax-deferred
- Taxed as income upon withdrawal
- Best when marginal rate at contribution > rate at withdrawal

Best Uses:
- High income earners (30%+ marginal rate)
- Long-term retirement savings
- When employer matches contributions
- Pension Adjustment reduces room

Withdrawal Rules:
- Can withdraw anytime (but lose room permanently)
- Added to income (taxed at marginal rate)
- Withholding tax applied: 10% up to $5K, 20% $5K-$15K, 30% over $15K
- Exceptions: Home Buyers' Plan, Lifelong Learning Plan

TFSA vs RRSP Decision Matrix:

Choose TFSA when:
- Current marginal tax rate < 30%
- Expect higher income in retirement
- Need flexibility to access funds
- Already maxing RRSP
- Saving for non-retirement goals

Choose RRSP when:
- Current marginal tax rate > 30%
- Employer matches contributions
- Expect lower income in retirement
- Committed to long-term retirement saving
- Want immediate tax refund

Ideal Strategy:
- Max RRSP to get employer match
- Build emergency fund in TFSA
- Max RRSP if high income
- Then max TFSA
- Then taxable account

Investment Vehicles:

1. Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs):
   - Trade like stocks on exchange
   - Low management fees (0.03% - 0.75%)
   - Instant diversification
   - Examples: VGRO, XEQT, VFV

2. Index Funds:
   - Mutual fund tracking specific index
   - Slightly higher fees than ETFs (0.1% - 1%)
   - Auto-invest available
   - Examples: TD e-Series, Vanguard funds

3. Mutual Funds:
   - Actively managed by fund manager
   - Higher fees (1.5% - 2.5%)
   - May underperform index over time
   - Common in employer plans

4. Individual Stocks:
   - Direct ownership in companies
   - Higher risk, higher potential return
   - Requires research and monitoring
   - Not recommended for beginners

5. Bonds and GICs:
   - Fixed income, lower risk
   - GICs: guaranteed return (1-5%)
   - Bonds: can be sold before maturity
   - Good for stability and short-term goals

6. REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts):
   - Invest in real estate without buying property
   - Monthly distributions
   - Diversification from stocks/bonds
   - Examples: Canadian Apartment REIT, RioCan

Asset Allocation:

Risk Tolerance Assessment:
- Time horizon (years until need money)
- Emotional capacity (can you stomach losses?)
- Financial capacity (can you afford losses?)
- Recovery ability (can you make up losses?)

Common Allocations by Age:

Age 20-30: 90% Stocks / 10% Bonds
- Long time horizon
- Can weather volatility
- Maximize growth potential

Age 30-40: 80% Stocks / 20% Bonds
- Still long time horizon
- Slight risk reduction
- Balance growth and stability

Age 40-50: 70% Stocks / 30% Bonds
- Moderate time horizon
- Preserve some gains
- Reduce volatility

Age 50-60: 60% Stocks / 40% Bonds
- Nearing retirement
- Protect accumulated wealth
- Generate income

Age 60+: 50% Stocks / 50% Bonds
- In retirement
- Preserve capital
- Generate stable income

Rule of Thumb: 100 - Your Age = % in Stocks
(Adjust based on personal risk tolerance)

Geographic Diversification:
- 30% Canadian market (home bias)
- 40% US market (largest economy)
- 30% International (Europe, Asia, emerging markets)

Sector Diversification:
- Technology, Healthcare, Financials
- Energy, Real Estate, Consumer goods
- ETFs do this automatically

Rebalancing:
- Review annually or when allocation shifts 5%+
- Sell winners, buy losers (counterintuitive but effective)
- Tax implications: do rebalancing in TFSA/RRSP when possible

Investment Strategies for Students:

1. Start Small:
   - Even $50/month compounds significantly
   - $100/month at 7% = $119,000 in 30 years
   - Don't wait for "enough" money

2. Automate Everything:
   - Pre-authorized contributions
   - Remove emotion from process
   - Dollar-cost averaging (buy more when prices low)

3. Low-Cost Index Approach:
   - Buy broad market ETFs
   - VGRO (80/20) or VEQT (100% equity)
   - Set and forget for decades

4. Avoid Common Mistakes:
   - Don't try to time the market
   - Don't check portfolio daily
   - Don't panic sell in downturns
   - Don't chase hot stocks or crypto

5. Tax Optimization:
   - Canadian dividends in taxable account (tax credit)
   - US stocks in RRSP (no withholding tax)
   - Bonds in RRSP (interest taxed as income)
   - Growth stocks in TFSA (tax-free gains)

Compound Growth Examples:

$500/month at 7% annual return:
- 10 years: $87,000 (contributed $60,000)
- 20 years: $262,000 (contributed $120,000)
- 30 years: $607,000 (contributed $180,000)
- 40 years: $1,310,000 (contributed $240,000)

Key Insight: Last 10 years nearly doubles your wealth
Early contributions have outsized impact

Common Questions:

Q: Should I invest or pay off debt?
A: Pay off high-interest debt (>6%) first, then invest

Q: What if the market crashes after I invest?
A: Keep investing - buying more shares at lower prices

Q: Do I need an advisor?
A: Not necessary with index funds; robo-advisors if you want help

Q: How much should I have invested by age 30?
A: 1x annual salary recommended

Q: Can I lose money in my TFSA/RRSP?
A: Yes, if investments decline. Accounts don't protect from losses.
